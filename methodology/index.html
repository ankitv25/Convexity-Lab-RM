Accounting boundaries

  • Five public source books remain five distinct funds.
  • Source inception, August reporting, private owner NAV, historical backtests and paper-strategy NAV are separate concepts.
  • A normalized base of 100 is an index—not $100, $100,000, a deposit or a transaction.
  • No fills, holdings, fees, flows, alpha or option exposure are invented from a NAV chart.

How to read the statistics

Return: ending source NAV divided by the reporting opening NAV, minus one. August uses the preceding available close; inception uses the source’s opening base of 100. The first day’s movement is retained.

Reference: the source-normalized benchmark for that fund. Balanced references compound their daily weighted equity/bond return, not an average of cumulative endpoints.

Drawdown: NAV divided by its selected-window running maximum, minus one. The running maximum includes the opening reporting base.

Beta: sample covariance of daily fund and reference returns divided by reference-return variance. Near-zero reference variance makes beta unavailable.

Volatility and tracking error: sample standard deviation of daily fund and active returns, respectively, multiplied by √252. These are annualized descriptions of a short sample, not forward volatility forecasts.

Monthly return: compounded daily returns within the displayed month. A partial month is not presented as a completed month.

Withheld: this public slice does not estimate manager alpha, Sharpe, Sortino, a Greek exposure, holdings attribution or calibrated probabilities. Those require additional validated inputs and evidence.

Publication boundary

The public site contains rendered charts, approved source observations and display controls. No uploaded holdings, owner ledger, saved strategy, authentication configuration or private calculation source belongs in its artifact.
